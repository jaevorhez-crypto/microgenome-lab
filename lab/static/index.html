Identify a sequence

Paste DNA or RNA (FASTA is fine). Your GPU compares it with every indexed RefSeq viral genome and 16S rRNA sequence and ranks the closest ones, with their taxonomic lineage.

DNA double helix

An idealised B-DNA helix (3.38 Å rise, 34.3° twist) built from a real sequence. The coordinates are computed from the sequence and your GPU draws the picture. Leave the box empty to use the real phiX174 genome, or paste your own bases.

Open the interactive 3D viewer
Press Render.

Proteins

Real structures from the public Protein Data Bank, drawn on your GPU. Space-filling shows every atom (carbon coloured by chain, nitrogen blue, oxygen red, sulfur yellow); ribbon shows the backbone with helices and sheets thicker.

Drag to rotate, wheel to zoom, shift+drag to move, double-click to stop or start the spin.

Evolution: family trees

Pick a group (any name from the taxonomy, such as Betacoronavirus, Herpesviridae, Escherichia, Enterobacteriaceae, Bacillus) and the lab builds a distance tree from the sequences' sketches. You can add your own sequence as one more leaf and see where it falls.

Press Build tree.

Assembly: rebuild a genome from reads

A sequencer produces millions of short reads; assembly stitches them back into the genome. Simulate reads from a real genome (with sequencing errors) and see how well it is rebuilt, or paste your own reads (FASTA or FASTQ).

Bacterium (schematic)

A cutaway of a rod-shaped bacterium about 2 × 0.5 µm: layered envelope, folded DNA nucleoid, ribosome grains, flagella and pili, all drawn on your GPU. This is an illustration of how a cell is organised, not a measured structure.

Press Render.

Genome map: E. coli K-12

The whole 4.6-million-base genome as a circle, from the real NCBI sequence. Candidate genes are found by scanning all six reading frames; GC content and skew are measured in 3,600 windows. Drawn on your GPU.

Press the button.

Align two sequences

Exact global alignment with affine gaps, computed exactly, on your own computer. It reports the score, the CIGAR string and every SNP, insertion and deletion. Up to 3,500 bases each.

Sequence tools

Length, base composition, GC content, reverse complement, and the longest candidate open reading frames with their protein translation.
